# Worm CYOA V6.1 — Critical Review (Second-Model Pass)

**Reviewer's note:** This document is an adversarial review of the earlier analysis docs in this directory (`trump-stack.md`, `blindspots.md`, `matchups.md`, `synergies.md`, `archetypes.md`, `legalistic.md`, `hidden-gems.md`, etc.). The earlier docs were generated by a heuristic script with hand-curated S/A overrides — a method that is efficient but prone to **(a)** confirmation bias toward the analyst's favorite picks, **(b)** canon errors when the analyst hasn't read the actual CYOA text carefully, and **(c)** missing negative clauses buried in description text.

I went back to the source JSON and read the actual `text` fields for the most-cited picks. **Roughly a third of the prior analysis contains material errors.** This review catalogs them, then offers a more skeptical take on the meta.

## §1.0 — Material errors in the prior analysis (with evidence)

### §1.1 — Path to Victory HAS explicit blindspots (legalistic.md §2.2 is wrong)

**Prior claim** (`legalistic.md` §2.2, `trump-stack.md`, README): "CYOA removes the explicit blindspot" / "no blindspots in the Worm setting."

**Actual text** (`Path to Victory`, Combo, 15 SP):
> "You can come up with a step-by-step plan to fulfill any goal, and can perform each step perfectly. **Blindspots include Eidolon, Trigger Events, Entities, Parts of Entities, Endbringers, etc.**"

**Verdict:** **WRONG.** The Combo *explicitly enumerates* the same blindspots as canon Contessa. You cannot path around Eidolon, Endbringers, Entities, or Trigger Events. This invalidates the central premise of `trump-stack.md` Phase 3 ("PtV tells you which cape to copy") — PtV literally cannot see Eidolon, so it cannot tell you how to beat him.

**Fix needed:** PtV is a *planning* tool for situations you can already perceive, not an *omniscience* tool. It plans the steps once you've defined the goal; it doesn't reveal the battlefield.

### §1.2 — Oberon/Titania has a 5-active-power limit (trump-stack.md is wrong)

**Prior claim** (`trump-stack.md` §"Oberon/Titania lock"): "Permanent copy. Once copied, it's yours forever." (implying unlimited copies)

**Actual text** (`(Trump) Oberon/Titania`, Tier 1, 35 SP):

**Verdict:** **WRONG.** Oberon is hard-capped at 5 active copies. The "permanent" refers to duration (no time limit), not count. The Trump-stack math needs revision: with Oberon you get 5 powers, not "any number."

**Hidden upside missed by prior analysis:** Oberon works on **Cauldron vials and the Garden of Eden** — meaning you can copy powers from formula-derived capes (Cauldron capes) and from Eden's own garden. This is a massive canon hook the prior analysis missed entirely.

### §1.5 — Power Manipulator is far stronger than claimed (trump-stack.md under-rates it)

**Prior claim** (`trump-stack.md` #4, `blindspots.md`): "Power Manipulator: 1 active slot, 50% strength. Spend 1 hour copying."

**Actual text** (`(Trump) Power Manipulator`, Tier 2, 20 SP):
> "You have the ability to copy extremely weakened versions of powers off Parahumans and a slot to use those powers with. **To copy a Parahuman's power you must get within a meter of them.** **If you copy the power of an Endbringer or Entity, you get every single power they have separately, instead of just one power.**
> You can switch what power is in your slots at will. **Every day you get a charge you can use to increase the strength of a power**, though you can save these charges as well. **Once a power reaches the same strength as the original power, you can no longer dump charges into it.**
> On average it takes a week to fully charge a weak power, two weeks to fully upgrade a mid-level power, three weeks to fully upgrade a high-level power, and four weeks to fully upgrade an extremely powerful power.
> **You can also use those charges to increase how many slots you have, to a maximum of three.** It takes 30 charges to fully increase the number of slots you have..."

**Verdict:** **UNDER-RATED by a wide margin.** Power Manipulator is arguably the single strongest Trump in the CYOA because:
1. **1-meter range, not touch** — easier than FQ/Oberon
2. **Can copy Entities and Endbringers** — explicitly stated, gives *every* power separately
3. **Charges scale copies to 100% strength** — not stuck at "weakened" forever
4. **3 slots at max** — same as FQ, but no kill required
5. **No "worthy opponent" or "ghost projection" downsides**

The only real cost is time (1-4 weeks per power to fully charge). In a long campaign, PM outclasses FQ because it doesn't require murder and works on Entity-class targets.

**Fix needed:** PM should be co-#1 with FQ in the Trump priority list, not #4.

### §1.6 — Khepri is Tier 3, not Combo (multiple docs wrong)

This power extends through portals. You will retain your sanity. You can turn this power off at will, or let people within your range keep control of themselves while you control others. **Certain people are immune to this power, such as Alexandria and Imp.**"

**Verdict:** **WRONG on multiple counts.**

3. "Extends through portals" — but there's no Doormaker in the CYOA, so this clause is mostly inert unless you find a portal-creator in-world.
4. **Alexandria and Imp are immune** — canon-accurate but limits utility.

**Fix needed:** Re-categorize as Tier 3, drop the "anti-Endbringer silver bullet" claim. It's a close-range Master with hard counters.

### §1.7 — Fairy Queen requires KILL, has ghost-projection form (multiple docs imprecise)

**Prior claim** (`trump-stack.md`): "FQ requires a touch kill. Stored capes can be touched for Oberon copy. Recursion: 3 FQs × 3 slots = 9 capes."

**Actual text** (`(Trump) Fairy Queen`, Tier 2, 25 SP):
> "You have Glaistig Uaine's powerset. **With a touch you can kill a Parahuman and steal their power. You can steal their power from their corpse.** You can only have three active stolen powers at a time, and **all active powers take the form of a ghostly projection that somewhat resembles their living form, if in the form of a caricature.**"

**Verdict:** Prior analysis was directionally right but **missed key clauses:**
1. **Must kill.** No "store and release" mechanic — the cape dies, full stop.
2. **Ghostly projections.** The stolen powers manifest as ghosts, not as your own abilities. This has gameplay implications (ghosts can presumably be dismissed/banished by enemy Thinkers/Trumps; ghosts may have physical presence that can be attacked).
3. **Can steal from corpses.** So you can scavenge battlefields — you don't have to be the killer. (Under-rated by prior analysis.)

**On recursion:** The text says "steal their power" of a "Parahuman." A ghostly projection of a dead cape is arguably **not a Parahuman** anymore — it's a shade. The recursion claim ("FQ the FQ-stored") is much weaker than prior analysis asserted. The most defensible reading: **you can FQ living parahumans only.** Recursion is questionable.

## §3.0 — Where I disagree with the prior analysis's strategic conclusions

### §3.1 — The "Trump Stack" is over-rated as a build archetype

The prior analysis treats the Trump stack (FQ + Oberon + Eidolon + PM + Power Sight + Powerswap) as the strongest build. **I disagree.** Here's why:

**Problem 1: PtV blindspots break the planning loop.** The trump-stack's whole premise is "PtV tells you which cape to copy and how." But PtV can't see Entities or Endbringers — the actual end-game threats. Your trump-stack is blind to the things that matter most.

**Problem 2: FQ requires murder.** The prior analysis treats FQ as "free power storage." But every FQ use is a murder. In a Worm setting, that makes you a kill-order cape within a week. PRT, Protectorate, and S9 all come for you. The "FQ Eidolon" plan requires finding and killing Eidolon — who is the strongest cape in the setting and is itself a PtV blindspot to you.

**Problem 3: Power Manipulator outclasses FQ for patient builds.** PM doesn't require killing, has 1m range (vs FQ's touch), can explicitly copy Entities and Endbringers, and scales copies to 100% with daily charges. The only cost is time (1-4 weeks per power). In a multi-year campaign, PM is strictly better than FQ.

**Problem 4: Oberon's 5-cap means the "permanent copies forever" fantasy is false.** You get 5 active copies. That's strong, but it's not "1 Oberon + 3 FQ slots = 9 capes." It's "5 powers total, ever, swap by dismissing."

**My re-rating:** The trump-stack is A-tier as an archetype, not S-tier. The strongest archetype is **PtV + All Seeing Precognition + Sting + a defensive Tier 1** — a "precise answer" build rather than a "copy everyone" build.

### §3.2 — The "I-Win button" reframe

The prior analysis lists "I-Win buttons" as Oberon, FQ, PtV, Immortal, It Gets Worse. **I'd re-rank:**

1. **Determination Mode wish** — only true I-Win. Costs 100 SP and the whole build is flavor.
2. **PtV + All Seeing Precognition** — 15 + 20 SP + 10 CP = 35 SP + 10 CP. With no blindspots, PtV is true omniscience-in-planning. This is the second-best I-Win.
3. **Sting (Tier 1)** — 25 SP. The actual answer to Entities, Endbringers, and any defensive stack. Apply to any power you manifest.
4. **Power Manipulator** — 20 SP. Long-term scaling Trump that beats FQ on every axis except initial burst.
5. **(Trump) Eidolon** — 20 SP. 3 random powers, swappable, **you are a precognitive blindspot to everyone but the entity.** That last clause is huge and was under-emphasized.

The prior list (Oberon, FQ, Immortal, It Gets Worse) is full of powers with explicit holes. The real I-Win list is shorter and meaner.

### §3.3 — The "Shardless" archetype is a trap

Already covered in §1.3. Archetype #6 in `archetypes.md` should be deleted or rewritten as a challenge-run joke. The Mundane shard + shardless one-pointers produces a regular human with cold-reading and perfect aim. That's not a min-max build; it's a cosplay.

### §3.4 — The "Wish Gambler" archetype is dishonest

Archetype #1 in `archetypes.md` says "the build doesn't matter, the wish is the win." **This is rules-lawyerweasel wording.** The wish triggers "upon victory." If your build can't achieve victory, you don't get the wish. So the build DOES matter — it just needs to be viable enough to win, at which point the wish is the cherry.

**Fix:** The Wish Gambler should explicitly say "build a minimum-viable combat build (PtV + Sting + Immortal), win one fight, wish for omnipotence." That's an actual strategy. The prior framing reads like the wish is free, which it isn't.

## §4.0 — Things the prior analysis missed entirely

### §4.1 — The Eidolon "you are a blindspot" clause

`Eidolon` text: "You're a precognitive blindspot to everyone but the entity."

**This is enormous and was under-emphasized.** Picking Eidolon makes you invisible to Contessa, Dinah, Tattletale-style thinkers, and presumably PtV (the Combo). Combined with Suicide/Det Mode's "immune to precognition," you become completely unpathable. The trump-stack analysis should have led with this.

### §4.2 — Point Conversion as a budget multiplier

The Point Conversion row (idx 21) has 12 options for converting SP↔CP at varying rates:
- +25 SP -50 CP (you get 25 SP, lose 50 CP)
- +15 SP -30 CP
- +10 SP -20 CP
- ... down to +1 SP -1 CP
- And reverse: +25 CP -50 SP, etc.

**This is a budget multiplier the prior analysis didn't optimize.** If your build is SP-heavy (most powers cost SP), take "+25 SP -50 CP" to convert CP into SP at 2:1. If CP-heavy (skills/identity), reverse it. The prior budget math treated SP and CP as independent, which leaves value on the table.

### §4.3 — The "Genius of Hard Work" drawback is a power cap

`Genius of Hard Work` (-20 SP): "All of your powers that are more powerful, by default, than a power that would receive a PRT Threat Rating of 5 are weakened until they're at the level of powers with such a Threat Rating..."

**This is not free money.** It's a hard cap on your power ceiling. Taking this with Eidolon, Fairy Queen, or Oberon neuters them. Prior analysis listed it as a "best drawback" — wrong.

### §4.4 — (Brute) Endbringer has a core-kill mechanic

`Endbringer (Brute)`: "If the core is destroyed, you die."

**This is an explicit weakness.** Any Trump with Power Sight + Sting (or PtV) can find the core and kill you. Prior analysis treated Endbringer-tier powers as pure upgrades.

### §4.5 — Cloak perks stack with Suicide/Det Mode for layered Thinker defense

`Cloak Mark IV` (2 SP + 1 CP): "You are a blind spot to Thinker powers that get someone's information using anything other than environmental data or what the Thinker already knows."

Combined with Suicide/Det Mode's blanket precog immunity, this creates a layered defense:
- Layer 1: Precog immunity (Suicide/Det) — defeats PtV, Dinah, Coil
- Layer 2: Cloak IV — defeats Tattletale, Number Man (info-deduction Thinkers)
- Layer 3: Cloak V2/V3 (free with Suicide/Det) — defeats the remaining Thinker categories

The prior analysis mentioned Cloaks but didn't articulate the layered-defense model.
